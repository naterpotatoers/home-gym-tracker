# The coaching lens

Three influences the user follows, what each characteristically pushes for,
and where the evidence backs, tempers, or contradicts it. Tag claims that
rest only on these as **[influence]**. Keep attributions to their
well-known public positions — don't invent quotes or specific numbers and
pin them on a person.

## Squat University — Dr. Aaron Horschig (PT, DPT, CSCS)

Physical-therapist-run channel/book (*The Squat Bible*, *Rebuilding Milo*).
The PT half of the lens.

- **Screen, then load.** Check the joints that limit a pattern before
  blaming technique: ankle dorsiflexion (knee-to-wall test, compare sides),
  hip flexion/rotation, thoracic extension. A restricted joint upstream
  shows up as a fault downstream (heels rising, knees caving, butt wink).
- **Joint-by-joint thinking:** ankles, hips and T-spine want mobility; knees
  and the lumbar spine want stability/control.
- **Pain is a signal.** Don't push through it and don't just rest it —
  modify: shorten range, slow tempo, lighten load, swap variant, then build
  back. Most people can keep training *around* an irritated area.
- **Tendons and joints like progressive load.** Isometrics to calm an
  irritable tendon; heavy-slow and eccentric work to rebuild it.
- **Single-leg and split-stance work** for asymmetries, hip stability and
  knee health; "knees over toes" is fine when tolerated and built up.
- **Prep before work:** mobilize what's restricted, activate, then rehearse
  the pattern with ramp sets.

Evidence check:
- Restricted dorsiflexion predicts patellar tendinopathy and associates with
  knee valgus [cohort — Backman & Danielson 2011; SR/MA — Lima 2018]; calf
  stretching adds only ~2–3° [SR/MA — Radford 2006] — so expect modest
  mobility gains, and load the range you have.
- Progressive loading is the active ingredient in tendon rehab more than any
  specific protocol [RCT — Beyer 2015; SR/MA — Murphy 2018]; isometric
  analgesia evidence is in patellar tendinopathy [RCT — Rio 2015], weaker
  elsewhere.
- Training-around-pain with a pain-monitoring model is supported
  [RCT — Silbernagel 2007].
- "Glute amnesia"/"activation turns muscles on" is lore — warm-ups help
  readiness, not by "waking up" muscles (see `docs/pt-exercise-references.md`).

## Jonni Shreve — IFBB Pro, "Mr. Tell It Like It Is"

Bodybuilder and online coach. The effort-and-execution half of the lens.

- **Effort is the stimulus.** Working sets taken genuinely close to failure,
  with clean form — not a lot of comfortable sets.
- **Execution over ego:** controlled eccentrics, full range of motion, feel
  the target muscle, leave the momentum out.
- **No junk volume:** fewer, harder sets beat a long list done half-hearted.
- **Consistency and honesty:** show up, track, and be straight about whether
  you're actually progressing.

Evidence check:
- Hypertrophy improves as sets get closer to failure, with diminishing
  difference at 0–2 RIR; strength is less sensitive [SR/MA — Robinson 2024;
  Refalo 2023]. → isolation/accessories RIR 0–2; compounds RIR 1–3
  (beginners 2–3) — failure on technical barbell lifts isn't needed.
- Loads from ~6 to ~30 reps build similar muscle when sets are hard
  [SR/MA — Schoenfeld 2017 low- vs high-load].
- Full ROM / long-muscle-length training is at least as good and often
  better for growth — e.g. full squats grew glutes and adductors more than
  half squats [RCT — Kubo 2019; SR — Pallarés 2021].
- More weekly sets → more growth, with diminishing returns
  [SR/MA — Schoenfeld 2017 dose-response; Pelland 2024]. Per-session there's a
  ceiling: past roughly 6–10 hard sets for one muscle in a session, extra sets
  buy little [SR-adjacent/consensus].

## AthleanX — Jeff Cavaliere (MSPT, CSCS)

PT and former pro-sports strength coach. The balance-and-athleticism half.

- **Pull at least as much as you push** — more for desk-bound people; face
  pulls and external rotation for shoulder health.
- **Posterior chain and imbalances:** glutes, hamstrings, upper back, and the
  "small" muscles (rotator cuff, serratus, lower traps) that keep joints
  healthy.
- **Train like an athlete:** single-leg strength, carries, rotation and
  anti-rotation, some power — not just mirror muscles.
- **Exercise selection protects joints:** choose variants that load the
  muscle without provoking the shoulder/knee/back; don't keep doing the one
  that hurts.

Evidence check:
- Push:pull "ratios" are [consensus] — no trial sets a magic number — but
  scapular retractor strengthening + pec stretching does improve
  shoulder/head posture angles [RCT — Harman 2005; Ruivo 2017], and cuff +
  scapular work is first-line for shoulder pain [SR — Kuhn 2009]. Posture
  itself is weakly linked to pain [Slater 2019] — sell balance as shoulder
  health, not pain prevention.
- Carries, power and single-leg work are well supported for older adults'
  function and falls [SR/MA — Sherrington 2019 Cochrane: balance/functional
  exercise cuts the rate of falls by about a quarter]; power training improves function in older
  adults [SR/MA].
- Face pulls have no dedicated EMG study [consensus] — supported by the
  rowing/horizontal-abduction literature.

## Where the three agree (lead with these)

1. Show up consistently; design for the schedule that's real.
2. Hard, well-executed sets; progress them on purpose.
3. Balanced patterns — squat, hinge, single-leg, push, pull, carry, trunk.
4. Respect pain: modify and keep training, don't ignore and don't just rest.
5. Small joint-protecting work (cuff, glute med, ankles) is part of the plan,
   not an afterthought.

## Topic notes used often in this gym

- **Glute focus:** use all three glute jobs — hip extension at short length
  (hip thrust, bridges), long-length hinge (RDL), and deep knee-dominant
  single-leg work (Bulgarian split squat, step-up). Hip thrust and squat
  produced similar glute growth over 9 weeks, the squat also growing quads
  and adductors [RCT — Plotkin 2023], so the hip thrust is a great tool, not
  a requirement or a replacement for squatting/lunging. Glute med:
  side-lying abduction and band-at-ankles lateral walks top the EMG list
  [EMG — Distefano 2009; Cambridge 2012].
- **One day a week:** with volume equated, 1×/week builds muscle similarly to
  2–3×/week [SR/MA — Schoenfeld 2019] — but one session can't hold unlimited
  volume, so prioritize 2–3 target muscles near the per-session ceiling and
  give everything else a maintenance dose. Maintenance is cheap: about a
  third of the original volume, at the same intensity, held strength and size
  in younger adults [RCT — Bickel 2011; review — Spiering 2021].
- **Runners who lift:** the interference effect is real but small for
  strength/hypertrophy and larger with running than cycling [SR/MA — Wilson
  2012]. Heavy strength + plyometrics improve running economy
  [SR/MA — Balsalobre-Fernández 2016; Blagrove 2018]. Keep the hardest leg
  session ≥48 h before the long run or key session [consensus]. Calf/soleus
  capacity matters — train straight- and bent-knee calf raises heavy
  [consensus, mechanistic]. Novices who ramp mileage >30% over two weeks get
  more distance-related injuries [cohort — Nielsen 2014].
- **Older adults (60+):** progressive resistance (eventually heavy, technique
  first), balance, power (fast concentric), carries, sit-to-stand; high-
  intensity resistance + impact training improved bone density in
  postmenopausal women with low bone mass [RCT — Watson 2018 LIFTMOR].
- **Ankle pain on calf raises:** regress, don't remove — bilateral, partial
  range, isometric holds, slower tempo, bodyweight before dumbbells; keep
  knee-to-wall mobility in; rebuild single-leg balance. Red flags (swelling,
  instability, night pain, >2–3 weeks) → clinician.
