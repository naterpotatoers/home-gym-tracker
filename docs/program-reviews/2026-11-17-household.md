# Program review — household, 2026-11-17

Coach + PT review of every program in the gym against what each person
actually did over the last 16 weeks (Aug 3 – Nov 17). Made with the
`program-review` skill (`.claude/skills/program-review/`); numbers come from
its digest script, whose coverage math matches the app's coverage panels
exactly. Lens: Squat University, Jonni Shreve, AthleanX — each checked
against the evidence (tiers in brackets).

> Not medical advice. Pain notes are coached around with a pain-monitoring
> rule; anything with red flags goes to a clinician.

## Headline

1. **Nobody but Nate is on a program.** Starter was 4 weeks long, and every

   (the app's "neglected" band), and she noted ankle pain during calf raises

   sets per session), assigned from Monday 11-23.
5. **Lift 3 + Run 3 is the best-built program in the gym.** The gap is the
   running: over the last 7 weeks, about 1.4 runs and 6.8 mi a week against
   a planned 3 runs and ~16.5 mi. There are also smaller holes in calf/soleus and elastic work.

## Lift 3 + Run 3 (Nate, week 2 of 6)

**What's working.** The week is sequenced the way the concurrent-training
evidence suggests [SR/MA — Wilson 2012; consensus]:
- Heavy squat Monday.
- The only heavy hinge Wednesday.
- Single-leg work at RIR 3 on Friday, so the Saturday long run is fresh.

The runner prehab is real: Nordics (hamstring strain risk roughly halved
[SR/MA]), Copenhagens, tibialis raises, Pallof press and dead bugs.

Push:pull is 9:14, with both planes covered. Every main lift has a rep range
and a written progression rule. Weekly coverage is solid for quads,
hamstrings, glutes, back and arms.

thrusts, a single-leg day, anti-rotation trunk work (Pallof), face pulls,
dead bugs, Copenhagens and clamshells. The bones are good.

**Why one day works:** with volume equated, 1×/week builds muscle about as
well as 2–3×/week [SR/MA — Schoenfeld 2019]. So the session spends its
volume on the two priorities near the per-session ceiling, and everything
else gets a maintenance dose, which is cheap [RCT — Bickel 2011].

**Trade-offs, on purpose:**
- Quads are light. The BSS covers them; add a goblet squat if she wants more.
- No vertical pull or press.
- No calves until the ankle settles.

**The ankle (PT lens).**
- No loaded calf raises for now.
- Do the knee-to-wall every session and note which side is tight or sore.
- Once two sessions in a row are pain-free, reintroduce calf work in this
  order: two-leg bodyweight raises in a partial range → full range → slow
  lowering → single leg → load.
- During that, discomfort up to 5/10 that's settled by the next morning is
  acceptable [RCT — Silbernagel 2007].
- If the front ankle complains in the split squat, swap to step-ups.
- **See a physical therapist if** there's swelling, the ankle gives way,
  it hurts walking or at night, or it isn't better in 2–3 weeks.

**Progression:** double progression on every row. Hit the top of the range
on all sets at the target RIR, then take the smallest load step: 2.5–5 lb on
the bar, the next dumbbell pair, or the next band rank.
